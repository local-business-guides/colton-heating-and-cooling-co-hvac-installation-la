What the installation process includes

Installation needs vary by property and system. A contractor will generally review the existing equipment and the requirements for the new system, discuss suitable options, and explain the work needed to put it in place. The job may also involve removing old equipment and checking that the new system operates as intended.

Key facts to keep in mind:
- Scope: Heating equipment, cooling equipment, or a system serving both needs may be part of the discussion.
- Timing: The schedule depends on the property, equipment, and work involved. Ask what assessment, preparation, installation, and follow-up steps are expected.
- Cost factors: The system selected, the size and layout of the property, existing equipment, and installation requirements can affect the overall cost. Ask for a written explanation of the proposed work and its cost before approving it.
- Provider comparison: Compare what each contractor recommends, what is included, how they explain system fit, and how they handle questions after installation. Don’t rely on equipment names alone.

A few common terms may come up. System capacity describes how much heating or cooling equipment can provide. Efficiency refers to how effectively a system uses energy. Ductwork is the network that carries conditioned air through a building, when the system uses ducts.
